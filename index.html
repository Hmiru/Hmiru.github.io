About

Who I am

I love football, and I support Everton. For a long time, football was only a game that I watched. Then it became a problem that I wanted to solve. A match is continuous and chaotic. I wanted to know how much of that chaos a computer can handle. The more I looked, the more possibility I saw.

If something does not work, I stay with it until it works. I prefer to finish a problem before I go to the next one. I also think about the way I explain my work. A result is not complete until other people can understand it in simple words.

Focus

Problems I want to solve

01An embedding that survives a change of club

I want one embedding for each player. But a player changes the game around them, and the game around them changes the player. Every sequence a player takes part in is a joint product of the two, so no single one of them is about the player alone. A player appears in thousands of sequences, with different teammates, different opponents and different states of the game. The player is the only thing that recurs while the rest moves. Disentangled representation learning is how I want to pull that recurring part out. If it works, the same embedding should still hold after a transfer, and it should say why two players make different choices in the same situation.

02One model for the ball and for the running

Event data records what happens to the ball. Tracking data records where everyone else is while it happens. The two are usually modelled apart, so a model learns either the decision or the movement, never both. I want a single model that generates the next event and the movement around it at the same time. The run that drags a defender out of position is part of why the pass was on, and a model that never sees the run cannot account for the pass.

03What a player does inside a different team

A transfer is the largest single cost for most clubs, and there is no correct answer to check it against. I want to put a player into a new lineup and let the match run, before the club pays. The same machinery answers the smaller questions too: whether a player suits a particular system, and what changes when the lineup changes. Aggregate statistics cannot do this. They say what a player did somewhere else. They do not say what happens here.

In the end, football is what the players do. If I can encode the brain and the feet of a player, I get a little closer to an answer.
